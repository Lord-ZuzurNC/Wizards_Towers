# Spirits and Guardians — Wizards Towers

## A place, or a person

A **guardian** holds a place and never leaves it. It is stationed in a village by a village upgrade — the physical result of a purchase a wizard made — and if that village falls, every guardian stationed in it falls with it.

A **spirit** follows a person and never holds ground. It travels with the wizard everywhere the wizard goes: through villages, dungeons, mazes and the Monolith alike, and it has no post of its own to defend.

The two categories never compete for the same slot, cap or resource. A guardian is bought through a village upgrade and paid for in village gold; a wizard who owns no villages can still carry a spirit, and a wizard who never leaves their capital gets no use from one. Filling every guardian slot in a village does nothing for the wizard personally, and carrying a spirit does nothing for a village the wizard is not currently near. Between them they cover the two different gaps created by the same fact: a wizard can only be in one place.

## Guardians

Guardians are the defensive units a village upgrade stations inside a village. They exist for one reason above all others: a wizard's **echo** defends only their **capital** village, and only while the wizard is offline — every other village a wizard owns has no echo at all, so guardians are the entire answer to how it survives a raid, by a rival wizard or by a roaming enemy, while nobody is watching. Guardians use the same six-stat block as every other combatant — `health`, `power`, `mana pool`, `magic defence`, `speed`, `luck` — and, like enemies, fight with their own authored attacks. A guardian never casts a spell and never touches the rune system, not even a guardian whose `Atk type` is `magic`.

Guardian `health`, `power`, `magic defence`, `speed` and `luck` are drawn from the same tier baselines and ±25 % tolerance band the bestiary uses for enemies — critter 30 health, common 80, veteran 200, elite 450, champion 900 — since a guardian is a combatant of comparable strength to the enemies it is built to repel. `mana pool` is assigned per entry below; the contract does not fix it by tier, for guardians any more than for enemies.

## Guardian deployment rules

A village's guardian cap is set entirely by its own Guardian Barracks tier, and that cap is **per village**, not per wizard: it is never shared with, drawn from, or reduced by any other village the same wizard owns. A wizard holding five villages buys five separate barracks and maintains five separate caps.

Within a single village, every guardian stationed there — whether holding the wall, the gate, or patrolling the crop fields outside it — draws from that same one cap. There is no separate crop-guardian pool: a village that spends its whole cap on Mounted Guardians and Harvest Watches has no capacity left for a Veteran Guardian at the wall, and the reverse is equally true.

Guardian Captain is the one exception to the cap itself rather than to the sharing rule: a village may station only one, regardless of its Barracks tier or how much capacity that tier otherwise grants.

A guardian is bound to the village that stationed it, never to the wizard who paid for it. A village that is **stolen** keeps whatever guardians were stationed in it before the theft; they do not follow their former owner, and they do not turn against the wizard who now owns the village. A village that is **destroyed** loses every guardian stationed in it along with the village itself, the same way a ruined tower loses its upgrades.

A guardian killed in a raid stays dead. It does not regenerate and is not automatically replaced — it must be rebought from the Barracks at that guardian type's current escalating price, a price that falls as guardians of that type are lost, the same escalating-price rule that governs every other purchase type. Sentinel Automaton's immunity is to seasonal decay specifically; it dies in a lost raid exactly like any other guardian.

⬜ Whether the per-village cap set by Guardian Barracks has a hard numeric ceiling, or keeps rising indefinitely at escalating gold cost.
⬜ Whether a guardian defeated while its owner is online and physically present behaves any differently from one defeated while the owner is offline.

## Spirits

A spirit is a small follower that accompanies a wizard. It is the only companion a wizard has, which makes it the natural home for small, persistent utility rather than for raw combat: carrying resources across distance, scouting ahead, warning of trouble a wizard cannot otherwise see, and — for a handful of entries only — helping directly in a fight. Most spirits below carry no stat block at all; they act on the world rather than fighting in it. `Tier` uses the same common / rare / legendary rarity scale as runes, since a spirit is found, tamed, bought or granted the same way a rune is, never bought through a village.

## Spirit rules

A wizard carries **exactly one spirit active at a time**, with no reserve of unused spirits waiting behind it. A spirit is the only companion a wizard has; holding more than one at once would turn that defining trait into a stacking slot, and would let a wizard carry every warning-type spirit simultaneously, erasing the very blind spots — a village raided while its owner is mid-dungeon, a floor contested while its owner is mid-raid — the category exists to cover one at a time.

A spirit carries no health in the general case and cannot be destroyed by combat the way a guardian can. Glimmer Fox and Thistlewarden are the only two with a stat block, and losing their own fight action only pauses that spirit's contribution for the remainder of the fight; it does not destroy the spirit.

Every spirit except one is run-scoped, exactly like gold, villages and runes, and is lost the instant its wizard dies, the same as everything else a wizard owns. Keepsake Wisp is the exception: bought once with reincarnation points as a talent, it crosses the wipe because talents are the one category of ownership built to survive a run's end.

An ordinary spirit is bound to the **run**, not to the wizard: it disappears on death along with every other run-scoped possession, and a wizard starting their next run has none until a new one is found, tamed, bought or quested for again. Keepsake Wisp alone is bound to the **wizard**, across every run.

⬜ Whether swapping an active spirit for a different one destroys the old one outright or merely benches it for later use.
⬜ Whether another wizard can target and destroy an enemy's spirit directly in combat, separately from attacking the wizard carrying it.

## Balance

**Guardians.**
🔴 Guardian Captain's aura has no stated cap on how many stationed guardians it can buff at once. In a village with a maxed Barracks full of Elite Guardians, the Captain's bonus stacks with Battle Formation's own coordination bonus, risking a single village no raider can ever profitably attack. Either cap the number of guardians the Captain buffs, or make the Captain and Battle Formation bonuses mutually exclusive.
🟡 Bastion Sentinel overlaps Elite Guardian: both are melee wall-holders one tier apart, and the mount bonus is the only thing separating Bastion Sentinel from a reskinned Veteran Guardian. Worth testing whether the mount bonus belongs on Veteran Guardian directly instead of as its own named entry.
🟡 Stone Legion Trooper and Militia Guardian serve the same cheap-and-numerous niche at the same tier with no behavioural difference; one of the two is probably redundant once their actual gold prices are set.

**Spirits.**
🔴 Watchwisp reaches its owner inside a dungeon or maze, the one place the game otherwise treats as cut off from the outside run. Firing with no limit removes the tension of being caught deep in a dungeon while a village burns rather than merely softening it; a cooldown or a delay on the warning would keep the spirit useful without erasing the stakes.
🔴 Stonepaws touches both mana-stones and life-stones in a single item, the two currencies every other document in this project treats as separately scarce. It likely needs splitting into two legendary spirits, one per resource, rather than shipping as one spirit that solves both shortages at once.
🟡 North Wall Herald and Ashbound Mote carry no mechanical effect at all, in the same vein as Trophy Hall and Capital Beacon among village upgrades; neither should take a development slot ahead of anything with an actual effect.
🟡 Harrowwisp overlaps Harvest Almanac: both forecast the next season, one at a village and one on the wizard's person. One of the two is probably redundant once a single season-forecast mechanic is chosen.
