## How to read this document

These are directions to try, not a finished style guide.
They will be confirmed or corrected by the first test sprites.

## Who makes the art

**Every piece of art is made by a person**: the author, or an artist sharing it for free or for a one-time payment.
No art is generated by AI.
The code, by contrast, is written entirely by AI.

Original art is licensed in `LICENSE-ASSETS.md`.
Third-party art is only taken under a free licence allowing commercial use for good, such as CC0, CC-BY or CC-BY-SA; it keeps its own terms, recorded in `CREDITS.md` and `license/`.
A CC-BY-SA piece that is modified must stay under CC-BY-SA, not under the project's licence.

## View and scale

| Choice             | Value                                                                                                   |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| View               | Top-down three-quarter, looking down at an angle                                                        |
| Tile               | 16 × 16 pixels                                                                                          |
| Pixel density      | One resolution everywhere: no pixel sprites over high-definition backgrounds                            |
| Speed 100          | 4 tiles, so 64 pixels, a second; see `WORLD_MAP.md`                                                     |
| Native resolution  | 640 × 360 for the whole screen, scaled by whole numbers: ×2 to 720p, ×3 to 1080p, ×4 to 1440p, ×6 to 4K |
| Drawing            | At screen resolution: an art pixel keeps its size, but things move by finer steps, so motion is smooth  |
| On screen          | 40 × 22.5 tiles; crossing the screen at speed 100 takes 10 seconds                                      |
| Characters         | 24 to 32 pixels tall                                                                                    |
| Large world bosses | 48 to 64 pixels tall                                                                                    |
| Frame rate         | Follows the screen's refresh rate, never capped; the simulation itself stays at 30 ticks a second       |
| Animation          | About 10 frames a second, independent of the game's frame rate                                          |
| Frames per action  | Idle 2 to 4, walk 4 to 6, spell or attack 4 to 6, death 4 to 8                                          |

## Palettes

**One palette for the game: BLK NX64**, 64 colours, for every character, spell, item, interface element and world tile.
Characters, spells and items travel between the world, dungeons and mazes, so they are drawn once, in this palette.

**COMFORT44S for the menus, the title screen and the ROOF.**
Its pastel tones set those screens apart; on the ROOF, only the environment uses it, while the wizards keep their own colours.

**APOLLO's 15 darkest colours reinforce BLK NX64 in dark rooms** of dungeons and mazes, those of lightness 30 or less out of 100; a lit room stays in BLK NX64 alone.
BLK NX64 alone runs short of dark tones there: in the test of `pre-POCs/dungeon_light`, the dim parts of a room keep 6 colours in BLK NX64, 14 once reinforced.

**Light stays within the palette.**
Darkness and light sources are computed, then every pixel on screen is snapped back to the nearest colour of the palette in use, light falling in visible steps blended by an ordered dither.

## Rooms and camera

| Place                                   | Size                                                                                      | Camera             |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Dungeon room, maze room, Monolith floor | One screen at most: 40 × 20 tiles, the rest of the screen holding the HUD                 | Fixed on the room  |
| The ROOF                                | A battle map of 3 × 3 screens, 120 × 60 tiles, which sudden death closes in from minute 5 | Follows the wizard |
| The open world                          | The whole map                                                                             | Follows the wizard |

Inside a dungeon or a maze the HUD is simplified, to leave the room as much of the screen as possible: health, mana, the spell bar, and a row of icons opening the menus, kept clear of the room.
A maze puzzle needing more room spreads over several rooms.

## Spell bar

**Two bars of five squares, one per row of keys** (`TECH_STACK.md`, *Controls*): the plain bar for Grimoire positions 1 to 5, the Shift bar for 6 to 10.
The squares follow the positions in order: left button, right button, Space, Q, E.

**The Shift bar sits behind the plain bar, a quarter of a square up and to the right.**
Three quarters of each of its squares are hidden, so it shows as an upturned L along the top and right of the plain bar.

**Holding Shift brings the Shift bar to the front; releasing it sends it back.**
Neither bar moves: only which one is drawn in front changes, so the eye never chases a moving bar mid-fight.
The bar in front is the active one, the one the keys cast from.

**Each bar shows its cooldowns on its own side: the plain bar's below its squares, the Shift bar's above.**
Both strips stay in view whichever bar is in front, so a slow spell in the Shift bar can be watched without holding Shift.
A cast square goes to a resting colour, its strip empties as the cooldown runs, and the square takes its colour back when the spell is ready.
BLK NX64 has no neutral grey: the resting colour is one of its dark blues.

**Above both bars, the five keys, named as the player's own keyboard prints them.**
Mouse buttons and Space are drawn as small icons, letters as letters.
They are blue while the plain bar is active and orange while Shift is held, both from the palette.

⬜ The size of a square and where the bar stands on screen, settled with the rest of the HUD.

## Look

Drawn from the references the author keeps privately, in an `inspiration/` folder left out of the repository:

- **Coloured outlines**: a darker shade of the object's own colour, not black.
- **Strong dynamic lighting**: torches, glows, and areas left truly dark, especially in dungeons and mazes.
- **An earthy base with saturated accents**, so what matters in a fight stands out: loot, spells, danger.
- **Vivid rather than faded colours.**
- **Never seen from the side, nor straight from above.**
