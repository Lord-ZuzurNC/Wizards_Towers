## How to read this document

These are directions to try, not a finished style guide.
They will be confirmed or corrected by the first test sprites.

## Who makes the art

**Every piece of art is made by a person**: the author, or an artist sharing it for free or for a one-time payment.
No art is generated by AI.
The code, by contrast, is written entirely by AI.

Original art is licensed in `LICENSE-ASSETS.md`.
Third-party art is only taken under a free licence allowing commercial use for good, such as CC0, CC-BY or CC-BY-SA; it keeps its own terms, recorded in `CREDITS.md` and `license/`.
A CC-BY-SA piece that is modified must stay under CC-BY-SA, not under the project's licence.

## View and scale

| Choice             | Value                                                                                                   |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| View               | Top-down three-quarter, looking down at an angle                                                        |
| Tile               | 16 × 16 pixels                                                                                          |
| Pixel density      | One resolution everywhere: no pixel sprites over high-definition backgrounds                            |
| Speed 100          | 4 tiles, so 64 pixels, a second; see `WORLD_MAP.md`                                                     |
| Native resolution  | 640 × 360 for the whole screen, scaled by whole numbers: ×2 to 720p, ×3 to 1080p, ×4 to 1440p, ×6 to 4K |
| On screen          | 40 × 22.5 tiles; crossing the screen at speed 100 takes 10 seconds                                      |
| Characters         | 24 to 32 pixels tall                                                                                    |
| Large world bosses | 48 to 64 pixels tall                                                                                    |
| Frame rate         | 60 frames a second for the game, so the camera scrolls about one pixel a frame at speed 100             |
| Animation          | About 10 frames a second, independent of the game's frame rate                                          |
| Frames per action  | Idle 2 to 4, walk 4 to 6, spell or attack 4 to 6, death 4 to 8                                          |

## Palettes

**One palette for the game: BLK NX64**, 64 colours, for every character, spell, item, interface element and world tile.
Characters, spells and items travel between the world, dungeons and mazes, so they are drawn once, in this palette.

**COMFORT44S for the menus, the title screen and the ROOF.**
Its pastel tones set those screens apart; on the ROOF, only the environment uses it, while the wizards keep their own colours.

**APOLLO in reserve for dungeons and mazes**, if BLK NX64 turns out short of dark tones there.
BLK NX64 has 10 colours darker than a lightness of 25 out of 100, pure black included; APOLLO has 13.

**Light stays within the palette.**
Darkness and light sources are computed, then every pixel on screen is snapped back to the nearest colour of the palette in use, light falling in visible steps blended by an ordered dither.

## Look

Drawn from the references the author keeps privately, in an `inspiration/` folder left out of the repository:

- **Coloured outlines**: a darker shade of the object's own colour, not black.
- **Strong dynamic lighting**: torches, glows, and areas left truly dark, especially in dungeons and mazes.
- **An earthy base with saturated accents**, so what matters in a fight stands out: loot, spells, danger.
- **Vivid rather than faded colours.**
- **Never seen from the side, nor straight from above.**
