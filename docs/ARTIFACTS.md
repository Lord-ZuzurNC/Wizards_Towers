# ARTIFACTS — Wizards Towers

An Artifact is a rune bound to a floor of the Monolith, not to a wizard.
Hold the floor, use the rune in your spell.
Lose the floor and the rune leaves your spell.

## How to read this document

This is the **catalogue**, not a single world's draw.
One hundred Artifacts are supposed to exist in the game; this document authors more than that on purpose, so the designer can prune it down.
When a world generates, it deals one Artifact to each of floors 1 to 49, so every world draws a different 49 from whatever the final catalogue becomes.
Extras beyond 100 are not waste — they are bonus material a future catalogue refresh can draw on without anyone writing new ones.

Each entry is a triple: the **attribute** it slots into, the **effect** it grants, and the **corruption** it costs.
The effect must do something an ordinary rune cannot, not a bigger number on something an ordinary rune already does.
The corruption is paired with the power, never a free tax — the designer's rule is that a corruption which **drains** a stat or a resource must have a minimum where the drain stops, so it can never reduce a wizard to zero or make them unplayable.
A corruption of any kind is cured by the priest at the Monolith's Ground Floor for one life-stone.

Every table has five columns.

| Column           | Meaning                                                                                 |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Name**         | World-unique, two to four words, never a glossary term on its own.                      |
| **Band**         | Which floors may deal this Artifact: normal (1-24), enhanced (25-39), powerful (40-49). |
| **What it does** | The effect, in the terms this project already uses for stats and systems.               |
| **Corruption**   | The cost, stated with its minimum where it drains anything.                             |
| **Notes**        | Synergy, intended use, or a warning about the combination that breaks it.               |

The five groups below mirror the five rune attributes: Shape, Range, Element, Duration, Effect.
Effect is the largest group, because it already is in `RUNES.md`, and because "what the spell does" is where the strangest ideas belong.

## Notes for balancing

**Entries I think read as overpowered as written.**
`The Last Life-Stone` is a free, automatic full heal once per life with almost no real downside beyond needing a stone on hand, and a wizard who plans ahead always has one; consider making the trigger threshold low enough that it is rarely free.
`Grave Standing` has a corruption that costs nothing while the caster is alive, so there is no real decision to make when slotting it — every wizard takes free insurance.
`The World's Memory` and `The Reincarnated Grudge` both assume a persistent, per-wizard record of who killed whom across lives, which is a real feature, not a flag, and may be more swingy in a duel than the rest of this catalogue if that record turns out to be easy to farm.

**Entries I think read as underpowered as written.**
`The Humble Petition` only works below three floors held, which is a narrow window most competitive wizards pass through quickly; it may need to scale back in rather than switching off.
`The Priest's Shortcut` depends on carrying a life-stone, and life-stones are meant to be rare (two or three a run), so its uptime is low enough that it may be a trap pick rather than a real cure path.
`The Unbroken Null` is deliberately the weakest Element in the catalogue by design, and should stay that way if kept, rather than be quietly strengthened in a balancing pass.

**Entries that depend on an undecided design question, marked where they appear above.**
⬜ `The Maze-Breaker` assumes mazes have a well-defined "solved state" a single hit can trigger; this depends on how mazes end up built, which `GLOSSARY.md` marks open at 32.5.
⬜ `The Permanent Record` and `The Reincarnation's Scar` both assume a dead wizard's run still banks something countable; `GLOSSARY.md` marks whether a dead wizard's score banks at all as open at 32.4.
⬜ `The World's Memory` additionally assumes cross-life kill records are tracked at all, which no current document commits to.
⬜ `The Re-Raider's Breath`, `The Re-Raider's Oath` and `Keeper's Grudge` all assume a re-raid can be lost in a way that matters; whether a re-raid fight can be fled is marked open in `GLOSSARY.md` at 32.2, and if it can be fled cheaply, all three lose most of their stakes.
⬜ `Last Page Standing`, `The Mana-Stone Gambit` and `The Hoarder's Lingering` all lean on the exact mana-stone price of editing a spell; whether swapping a single rune inside an occupied spell costs a stone, separately from replacing the whole spell, is marked open at 32.1.
⬜ `Contested Ground`, `Stonewarden's Mark` and `The Last Hit's Promise` all assume floor ownership is decided by a tracked highest-damage contribution rather than a simple last hit; this matches the direction `ASK.md` round 5 settled on, but whether the Monolith can lose a floor through any means other than a raid is still marked open at 32.3, and a different rule there would undercut all three.

**A deliberate pattern worth keeping, not a defect.**
Several corruptions mirror the same cost back onto the caster — `Second Mortgage`, `The Debtor's Chill`, `The Debt Collector` — rather than inventing a separate penalty.
That is intentional: a corruption that punishes the caster with the same tool it just used against someone else reads as a fair trade rather than a tax, and it was easy to keep consistent once the first one was written.
